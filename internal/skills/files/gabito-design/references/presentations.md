# Presentations (PowerPoint, Keynote, slides)

Use together with the pptx skill that writes the file. A slide supports a
speaker or a reader; decide which, because the design differs.

## Principles

- **One idea per slide.** The title is the claim, written as a sentence ("Support
  tickets fell 31 percent after the redesign"), not a topic ("Results").
- **Decide the audience mode.** Presented slides: few words, large type, visuals
  carry meaning, notes carry detail. Read-alone decks: denser, but still one idea
  per slide and clear hierarchy.
- **Use the slide master.** Build layouts (title, section, content, two-column,
  image, quote, closing) once; set fonts, colors and placeholders there. Slides
  inherit; no free-floating text boxes where a placeholder exists.
- **A grid and safe margins.** Consistent margins (about 5 percent), shared
  alignment edges across slides, a baseline for the title position that never
  jumps between slides.
- **Type sizes for a room.** Title 32 to 44 pt, body at least 24 pt, captions and
  sources at least 14 pt. If it does not fit, split the slide or cut words.
- **Evidence, shown well.** One chart per slide, one takeaway in the title, the
  key bar or point highlighted, others muted. Label directly instead of using a
  legend when possible; see `data-charts.md`.
- **Images with purpose.** Full-bleed photography, a product screenshot, a diagram
  that explains. Crop with intent; never stretch; keep text over images on a
  solid or scrimmed region with real contrast.
- **Rhythm.** Alternate dense and sparse slides, section dividers, an occasional
  big number or quote. Avoid thirty slides of the same layout.
- **Structure.** Opening that frames the question, the argument in 3 to 5 parts,
  a closing with the decision or next step. Agenda slides only for long decks.
- **Speaker notes.** Put the script, sources and backup numbers in notes, not on
  the slide.

## Quality floor

- Contrast 4.5:1 for body, 3:1 for large text; check on a projector-like gray
  (washed out) mentally, not only on your monitor.
- No more than two typefaces and about three colors plus neutrals.
- Every slide has a title (for accessibility and navigation), a logical reading
  order, and alt text on meaningful images and charts.
- Consistent bullet style, capitalization, date and number formats.
- Animations only to reveal a sequence the speaker will narrate; never to decorate;
  transitions are plain or none.
- Sources cited for numbers, in small but legible type.
- Exported PDF matches the deck; fonts embedded or common.

## Layout recipes

- **Title slide:** title, one-line subtitle, speaker and date, a single visual idea.
- **Section divider:** large number or word, short phrase, lots of space.
- **Claim + evidence:** sentence title, chart or image taking two thirds, one
  annotation highlighting the point.
- **Big number:** the number at 96 pt or more, a plain-language label, a source.
- **Comparison:** two columns with equal structure, the winner emphasized by
  weight or color, not by size alone.
- **Process:** three to five steps in a row with a consistent icon style, or a
  diagram from `diagrams.md`.
- **Closing:** the decision requested or the next three steps, contact.

## Presentation anti-patterns

- Topic titles and bullet-point paragraphs; reading the slide aloud.
- Walls of 12 pt text; five levels of bullets; tables copied from a spreadsheet.
- Stock-photo handshakes, light bulbs, puzzle pieces and rocket ships.
- Clip-art icons in a mix of styles; emoji as bullets.
- Template defaults: the same blue gradient, the same shapes on every slide.
- 3D charts, pie charts with ten slices, rainbow palettes, chart junk.
- Different title position, font size or margin on each slide.
- Text over a busy photo with no scrim; low-resolution logos.
- Every slide with an animation; spinning transitions.
- Decorative shapes (circles, swooshes) with no function; drop shadows on text.
- Agenda, "Thank you" and "Questions?" slides in a six-slide deck.
- Invented statistics and placeholder logos presented as real.

## Check before handing over

Step through in slideshow mode from the back of an imagined room. Read only the
titles: do they tell the story? Verify fonts, images and the exported PDF. Say
what you could not check.
