# Images, logos, icons, posters and covers

Covers raster and vector visuals made or chosen by the agent: illustrations,
logos, icons, social images, posters, covers, thumbnails, backgrounds.

## Principles

- **Brief first.** Subject, message, audience, where it is seen (size, distance,
  dark or light surface) and what must remain legible at the smallest size.
- **One concept.** A strong image says one thing. Remove elements until removing
  more would break the idea.
- **Composition.** Establish a focal point, use the rule of thirds or a clear
  symmetry on purpose, leave space for text, lead the eye with lines and contrast.
  Crop with intent; keep margins and safe areas.
- **Value structure.** Check in grayscale: the focal point and the text hold
  without color. Strong value contrast outperforms strong hue contrast.
- **Color.** A limited palette of three to five colors derived from the brand or
  the subject; consistent light direction and temperature.
- **Style consistency.** One illustration language per set: stroke weight,
  corner radius, level of detail, perspective, palette.
- **Text on images.** One typeface, large enough at the final size, on a calm or
  scrimmed area with 4.5:1 contrast; never on the busiest part.
- **Formats.** Vector (SVG) for logos, icons and diagrams; photographic or painterly
  content as WebP/AVIF/JPEG; PNG for sharp UI captures; provide 1x and 2x.
- **Accessibility.** Alt text that conveys purpose; decorative images marked
  decorative; text in images duplicated in text.

## Logos and marks

- Works at 16 px and on a billboard: simple geometry, few shapes, no fine detail,
  no gradients required.
- Test on light, dark, color and one-color versions; define clear space and a
  minimum size; provide horizontal and stacked lockups and a standalone mark.
- Be specific to the name and the field; avoid a generic symbol (globe, swoosh,
  lightbulb, generic abstract orb) used by thousands of products.
- Typography is half the logo: choose or tune letterforms, kerning and weight;
  do not default to a system font.
- Convert text to outlines for delivery, keep the editable source.
- Never imitate an existing mark; avoid anything that resembles a known brand.

## Icons

- One grid (for example 24 px), one stroke weight, one corner radius, one fill
  logic (outline or solid) across the set; pixel-aligned strokes.
- Metaphors that users recognize; pair with a text label for anything non-obvious.
- Optical balance over mathematical balance; consistent visual size.

## Posters, covers and social images

- Hierarchy of three: the hook, the details, the action or source. Read at
  arm's length or thumbnail size.
- Big type, few words; the title is the image or sits against a strong image.
- Respect crop zones of each platform; keep key content in the center safe area.
- Use real photographs, real product shots or purpose-made illustrations when
  you can; state when an image is a placeholder.
- Include date, place or link only when the viewer needs them to act.

## Generated or sourced images

- Say how an image was produced or sourced and check its license; do not use
  images of real people without permission or a clear right.
- When generating, describe subject, composition, light, palette and style
  concretely; inspect the result for artifacts (hands, text, symmetry errors,
  inconsistent shadows) and regenerate or retouch.
- Keep the prompt or source file with the asset so it can be reproduced.

## Image anti-patterns

- Glowing neon gradients on dark backgrounds with floating orbs and a robot or
  brain for anything involving AI; circuit-board and binary-rain backdrops.
- Stock-photo clichés: handshakes, headset smiles, people pointing at a laptop.
- Photorealistic faces with warped features; garbled or invented text in images.
- Purple-to-blue gradients on everything; neon on black as the default look.
- Over-detailed logos that fail at small sizes; logos that need a gradient to read.
- Mixed icon styles (outline plus solid plus 3D) in one interface.
- Clip-art, bevels, drop shadows and lens flares used as decoration.
- Low-resolution or stretched images; visible compression halos; mismatched aspect ratios.
- Text over a busy region with no scrim; text too small for the viewing distance.
- Every image in the set with a different style, lighting and palette.
- Fabricated brand marks, awards, certifications or customer logos.

## Check before handing over

View at final size and at 64 px; in grayscale; on a light and dark surface. Check
license and alt text. State what is placeholder.
